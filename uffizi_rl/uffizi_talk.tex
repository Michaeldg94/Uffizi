% --- 5. Scaling + masking ---------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Scaling up: deep RL and action masking}
\body
The real museum breaks the table: ninety-eight rooms, $\sim$100 moves per step, hundreds of state
numbers. We swap it for a neural net (\emp{MaskablePPO}; PPO and DQN as controls).
\vfill
\begin{block}{Action masking: the rulebook}
From any room only a few moves are legal, yet the net has $\sim$100 outputs. We set every illegal move
to $-\infty$ before the softmax, so it gets exactly zero probability. No gradient is wasted on moves
that do not exist; this state-dependent action set is our single biggest design choice.
\end{block}
\vfill
\[ \pi(a\mid s)=\frac{e^{z_a}\,\mathbf{1}[a\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}{\sum_{b}e^{z_b}\,\mathbf{1}[b\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}. \]
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 6. The reframe ---------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The reframe: when to book, not where to walk}
\body
A visitor has a map and guide, so the route is a shortest path. The real decision is the
\emp{booking}, hard for three reasons walking never has:
\vfill
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.7em}
\item \emp{Uncertain:} you cannot see how fast the good slots fill.
\item \emp{Irreversible:} book late and the masterpiece is gone.
\item \emp{Delayed:} you act today; the payoff lands at check-in ($\gamma = 0.999$).
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}\emp{Baseline (as-is):} no booking, take masterpieces as found.\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}\emp{RAMA:} booked slots, pick a lead time of $1$, $7$, $21$ or $35$ days.\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

% --- 9. Algorithm comparison -----------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Which algorithm wins and why masking matters}
\bodydense
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{\fig{15_algorithm_comparison.png}}
\vfill
\raggedright
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.35em}
\item \emp{MaskablePPO} is best or tied in every cell and stable across seeds.
\item \emp{Masking is decisive exactly at the hardest cell:} at the packed tourist, unmasked PPO
      \emp{collapses} to \bad{2093}, below the no-booking baseline of $2680$, while MaskablePPO holds
      \good{3685}. At easier crowds the two agree; the gap appears only where precision matters.
\item \emp{DQN is crowd-fragile and high-variance} (its baseline falls $4527\!\to\!2044$): taking a max
      over noisy crowd values overestimates, the very bias the deterministic toy did not have.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% --- 10. Challenge 1 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{1: an unlearnable reward}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{The agent never left, squatting in one room until closing. Zeroing all reward for not exiting made
it \emp{worse}.}
{An all-or-nothing wipe is \emp{flat} almost everywhere, so the gradient is zero: nothing points to
the door.}
{Use a \emp{dense} egress signal: per-step pressure rising near closing and far from an exit, plus a
bonus for leaving.}
{Sparse rewards are correct but often unlearnable. Shape a slope the optimiser can climb.}
\end{frame}

% --- 11. Challenge 2 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{2: it could not see what it was deciding}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{The rule ``stay until satisfied, then move'' never stabilised; dwell times wobbled at random.}
{The right action depended on value already taken from the room, \emp{not in the state}: a hidden
variable, a POMDP not the MDP we assumed.}
{Add a per-room \emp{appreciation-progress} value to the state, so ``seen enough?'' becomes something
the policy can read.}
{The state must hold everything the optimal action depends on, or you solve a different problem.}
\end{frame}

% --- 12. Challenge 3 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{3: reward shaping that backfired}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{Well-meant terms backfired: a ``boredom'' penalty caused \emp{looping}, a harsh crowd penalty made a
\emp{fake art lover}.}
{Every term is a new objective to game (reward hacking): looping beat exiting, a crowded Botticelli
scored worse than skipping it.}
{\emp{Minimal} shaping: a \emp{gentle} crowd discount so a crowded masterpiece still beats skipping,
no boredom penalty, satiation alone stops lingering.}
{Ask of every term: what is the cheapest way to collect it? Is that what you want?}
\end{frame}

% --- 13. Challenge 4 (the best trick) ---------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{4: the do-nothing trap and the book-early trick}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{Given a free ``decline'' button, \emp{both} profiles declined all three masterpieces; book-early
stayed only \emp{half} learned.}
{Declining is safe now, booking pays only later: a do-nothing optimum; its reward lands hundreds of
steps away (credit assignment).}
{Make ``always books'' a \emp{type trait} with no escape hatch; add an \emp{immediate off-pace
penalty} proxying the distant cost.}
{A small immediate signal tracking a far-off reward makes the long-horizon lesson learnable: our most
decisive trick.}
\end{frame}

% --- 14. Multi-seed variance ------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{What five seeds revealed (and why we ran them)}
\body
A single training run can be lucky or unlucky, so a single number can mislead. We retrained every cell
under \emp{five} training seeds and report the mean and the spread across them.
\vfill
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2.6em}
\item \emp{The tourist is rock-stable}: essentially zero variance at every crowd level.
\item \emp{The art lover degrades as the museum fills}: the gain falls from $+39\%$ to $+29\%$ to $+20\%$.
\item \emp{The catch:} at \emp{maximum crowd} the art lover is \emp{bimodal}, four of the five seeds
      reach $\sim$\good{+41\%} but one \bad{collapses} below baseline, so the cell averages \warn{+20\%}
      with a wide error bar. We report the spread, not the lucky seed.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% --- 16. Scoreboard and conclusion -----------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{What worked, what did not, what is debatable}
\bodydense
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
{\good{\textbf{Worked best}}}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1.15em}
\item \emp{Action masking}, the biggest lever.
\item \emp{Reframing} navigation into booking.
\item \emp{An immediate proxy} for a delayed reward.
\item \emp{Exposing appreciation progress}.
\item \emp{Dense egress shaping}: a way out.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
{\bad{\textbf{Worked worst}}}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1.15em}
\item \emp{All-or-nothing exit wipe}: zero gradient.
\item \emp{A free decline button}: a do-nothing trap.
\item \emp{A harsh crowd penalty}: a fake art lover.
\item \emp{A boredom penalty}: perverse looping.
\item \emp{Greedy evaluation} of a stochastic policy.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vfill
\emp{Debatable:} the 60/30/10 split and the dwell and crowd calibration are modelling choices; the fill
curve is calibrated, not fitted; the as-is museum hides the real entry choice (book or queue).
\end{frame}

% --- A2. The as-is 8-number state -------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: the as-is state, eight numbers}
\body
The visitor's local view on the planned walk (natural units; the network sees each scaled to about
$[0,1]$):
\vfill
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.55em}
\item \emp{Room-value drain}: minutes absorbed vs the room's worth ($\sim$$3$ min per importance point).
\item \emp{Room value}: importance, $1$ to $10$.
\item \emp{Density}: how full the room is, percent of capacity.
\item \emp{On-target flag}: are you in the next planned must-see?
\item \emp{Plan completed}: share of must-sees finished.
\item \emp{Time of day}: minute of the $615$-minute day.
\item \emp{Egress slack}: spare minutes to reach an exit before closing.
\item \emp{Distance to next room}: doorways to the next must-see.
\end{enumerate}
\vfill
\centering \emp{At the door \texttt{A1}: $(0,\,0.22,\,0.30,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0.99,\,0.10)$.}
\end{frame}

% --- A3. The RAMA 25-number state -------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: the RAMA state, twenty-five numbers}
\body
A two-phase episode (a booking screen, then the walk), grouped:
\vfill
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.55em}
\item \emp{Booking position} ($3$): phase flag, which of $4$ decisions, lead chosen (days).
\item \emp{Booking screen} ($4$): slots free at lead $1$/$7$/$21$/$35$ days (out of $8$).
\item \emp{Time of day} ($1$): minute of the $720$-minute day.
\item \emp{Windows held} ($3$): booked clock-times for Botticelli, Leonardo, Raphael.
\item \emp{Check-ins} ($3$): which masterpieces collected.
\item \emp{Access read} ($4$): is-masterpiece, unbooked, open-now, minutes-to-open.
\item \emp{Pace prior} ($3$): when it expects to reach each masterpiece (frozen).
\item \emp{Room tail} ($4$): drain, value, density, egress, as in the as-is state.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\centering \emp{Opening screen: $(1,0,1;\ 0.12,0.38,0.62,1;\ 0;\ \dots)$; the booking screen is the crux.}
\end{frame}

% --- A4. Actions and masking ------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: actions and action masking}
\large
\vfill
\textbf{Actions per setting}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1.0em}
\item Toy / as-is: \texttt{Discrete(2)}, stay or move on.
\item RAMA: \texttt{Discrete(4)}. Booking phase: a lead $\in\{1,7,21,35\}$ or book/decline. Walk phase:
stay or move on.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\textbf{Masking (MaskablePPO).} Set the logits of illegal moves to $-\infty$ before the softmax, so they
get exactly zero probability:
\vfill
\[ \pi(a\mid s)=\frac{e^{z_a}\,\mathbf{1}[a\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}{\sum_{b}e^{z_b}\,\mathbf{1}[b\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}. \]
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- A5. Reward functions ---------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: the reward functions}
\normalsize
\vfill
\textbf{Toy, per step.}
$\dfrac{\text{importance}\times\text{novelty}}{1+\alpha_A\,\text{density}^2}-\text{step cost}$, with
$\alpha_A=6$, step $0.5$, exit bonus $+15$, no-exit penalty $-50$ (hand-tuned shaping; the relative
sizes are what matter).
\vfill
\textbf{Full museum, per minute.} importance $\times$ crowd discount $\times$ novelty (satiation), with
a gentle crowd $\alpha=0.5$, plus closing-time pressure, a completion bonus and a small step cost.
\vfill
\textbf{Booking terms (RAMA only).} an \emp{immediate off-pace penalty} at commit time (a local proxy
for the distant cost of a badly-timed slot) plus a no-show penalty for a masterpiece booked but missed.
\end{frame}

% --- A15. Training and evaluation setup -------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: training and evaluation setup}
\normalsize
\vfill
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
\textbf{Deep RL (Stable-Baselines3).} \texttt{MlpPolicy} $[128,128]$, Adam LR $3\times10^{-4}$,
$2048$ steps, batch $256$, $10$ epochs, GAE $\lambda=0.98$, clip $0.2$, entropy $0.01$.
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.9em}
\item As-is baselines (PPO): $150$k steps, $\gamma$ $0.995$/$0.997$.
\item RAMA arms: $100$k steps, $\gamma=0.999$.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Toy.} tabular Q / Double-Q, $\varepsilon$-greedy, $\gamma=0.99$, $25$k episodes.
\vspace{1.4em}

\textbf{Seeds and evaluation}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.9em}
\item $5$ training seeds $\{1,3,7,42,202606\}$.
\item Eval on $6$ fixed crowds (\texttt{900000} to \texttt{900005}), deterministic, common random numbers.
\item Crowd levels $500$ / $2500$ / $5000$ visitors a day.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vfill
\end{frame}
