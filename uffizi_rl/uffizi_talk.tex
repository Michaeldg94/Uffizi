% --- 5. Scaling + masking ---------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Scaling up: deep RL and action masking}
\footnotesize
\vfill
The real museum breaks the table: 98 rooms, $\sim$100 actions, a $\sim$400-dim state. So we
switch to neural networks and \emp{MaskablePPO}, with unmasked PPO and DQN as controls.
\vfill
\begin{block}{Action masking: hand the agent the rulebook}
The museum is a graph, so most actions are illegal at each step. We force every illegal action
to zero probability before the softmax. No gradient is wasted learning ``which moves exist'';
it all goes into the real decision.
\end{block}
\vfill
\begin{center}\emp{It will turn out to be decisive exactly where the decision is hardest.}\end{center}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 6. The reframe ---------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The decisive reframe: a booking problem}
\footnotesize
\vfill
We spent months on a \emp{navigation} agent. It was brittle and the worry grew: is routing
with a map even a real RL problem? \emp{It is not} (a visitor has a map and a guide).
\vfill
The genuine, uncertain decision is the \emp{booking}:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{8pt}
\item \emp{baseline (as-is museum):} no booking, just walk the crowds, the museum as it runs today;
\item \emp{intervened (RAMA):} masterpieces gated by booked time-slots, so the decision becomes
      \emp{lead time} $\in\{1,7,21,35\}$ days, then pace to each window.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
A crowd-dependent \emp{fill curve} makes good slots sell out on busy days, so booking late at a
packed museum misses masterpieces.
\vfill
\begin{center}\emp{Lesson: choosing the MDP is more than half the work.}\end{center}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 9. Algorithm comparison ------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Which algorithm wins and why masking matters}
\footnotesize
\vfill
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{\fig{15_algorithm_comparison.png}}
\vfill
\raggedright
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{5pt}
\item \emp{MaskablePPO} is best or tied everywhere, stable across seeds.
\item \emp{Masking is decisive at the hardest cell:} at the packed tourist, unmasked PPO
      collapses to \bad{2093} (below the 2680 baseline) while MaskablePPO holds \good{3685}.
\item \emp{DQN} is crowd-fragile and high-variance (baseline $4527\!\to\!2044$): its max over
      noisy crowd values overestimates, the bias the toy never had.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 10. Challenge 1 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The journey was the project: 1, an unlearnable reward}
\footnotesize
\vfill
\sdfl
{The agent never left the museum: it squatted in a room until the day ended. Zeroing all reward
for failing to exit made it \emp{worse}.}
{An all-or-nothing terminal wipe is \emp{flat} across almost every policy, so the gradient is
zero. Nothing points toward the door.}
{A \emp{dense} egress signal: per-step pressure that grows as closing nears, a finite (not
infinite) no-exit penalty, an exit bonus.}
{Sparse all-or-nothing rewards are often unlearnable. Shape a slope the optimiser can climb.}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 11. Challenge 2 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{2: the agent could not see what it had to decide on}
\footnotesize
\vfill
\sdfl
{``Stay until satisfied, then move on'' never stabilised; the agent over- and under-dwelt rooms
at random.}
{The right action depended on \emp{how much value it had already extracted here}, which was
\emp{not in the observation}, a hidden variable (a POMDP).}
{Add a per-room \emp{appreciation-progress} value to the state, so ``have I seen enough?'' is
something the policy can read.}
{The state must contain everything the optimal action depends on.}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 12. Challenge 3 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{3: reward shaping that backfired}
\footnotesize
\vfill
\sdfl
{A ``boredom'' penalty produced \emp{looping}; a harsh crowd penalty produced a \emp{fake art
lover} that skipped crowded masterpieces.}
{Every term you add is a new objective the optimiser games (reward hacking): circling beat
exiting; a crowded Botticelli scored worse than skipping it.}
{\emp{Minimal} shaping: a gentle crowd discount (a crowded masterpiece still beats skipping),
drop the boredom penalty, let satiation carry ``move on''.}
{Stress-test every reward term for the perverse incentive it creates.}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 13. Challenge 4 (the best trick) ---------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{4: the do-nothing trap and the trick that cracked book-early}
\footnotesize
\vfill
\sdfl
{Given a free ``decline'' button, \emp{both} profiles declined all masterpieces. Even without
it, book-early was only \emp{half}-learned.}
{Declining is immediately safe while booking pays later (a do-nothing trap); and the booking
payoff is hundreds of steps away, so its gradient is faint (credit assignment).}
{Treat ``always books'' as a \emp{type trait} and add an \emp{immediate off-pace penalty}, a
local proxy correlated with the distant cost.}
{A small immediate signal that tracks a distant reward is what makes a long-horizon lesson
learnable. The single most decisive trick.}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 14. Multi-seed honesty -------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{What five seeds revealed}
\footnotesize
\vfill
A single run can be lucky, so we retrained every cell under \emp{five} seeds and report the spread.
\vfill
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{10pt}
\item \emp{Tourist:} rock-stable at every crowd.
\item \emp{Art lover:} the gain degrades as the museum fills ($+39 \to +29 \to +20\%$).
\item \emp{The honest catch:} at \emp{max crowd} the art lover is \emp{bimodal}, four of five
      seeds reach $\sim$\good{+41\%} but one \bad{collapses}, so it averages \warn{+20\%} with a
      wide band. We report the spread, not the lucky seed.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\begin{center}\emp{Reporting variance honestly is part of the result.}\end{center}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 15. Scoreboard ---------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{What worked best and worst}
\footnotesize
\vfill
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\good{\textbf{Worked best}}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{8pt}
\item \emp{Action masking}: the single biggest lever.
\item \emp{Reframing to booking}: the right MDP.
\item \emp{An immediate proxy} for the delayed reward.
\item \emp{Exposing appreciation progress}: Markov again.
\item \emp{Dense egress shaping}: a learnable exit.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
\bad{\textbf{Worked worst}}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{8pt}
\item \emp{All-or-nothing exit wipe}: zero gradient.
\item \emp{Free decline button}: a do-nothing trap.
\item \emp{Harsh crowd penalty}: a fake art lover.
\item \emp{Boredom penalty}: a looping incentive.
\item \emp{Greedy eval} of a stochastic policy.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 16. Conclusion + limitations -------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Conclusion and what is debatable}
\footnotesize
\vfill
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{9pt}
\item \emp{Result:} the optimal art lover and tourist \emp{book early, earlier when busier},
      secure all three masterpieces, beat the no-booking baseline by \good{+20\% to +39\%} (art)
      and \good{+36\% to +38\%} (tourist).
\item \emp{The journey was the project:} a chain of instructive failures, each a named RL lesson,
      unlearnable reward, partial observability, reward hacking, do-nothing trap, credit assignment.
\item \emp{Honestly debatable:} the 60/30/10 split, the dwell and crowd-density calibration and
      room positioning are modelling choices (the floor-plan maps let you judge them); the fill
      curve is calibrated, not booking data; the as-is museum abstracts away the real entry choice
      (pre-book and skip the queue, or pay less and queue).
\item \emp{Next:} the Vatican Museums, a larger topology and real booking traces.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\end{frame}
