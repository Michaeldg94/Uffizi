% --- 3. The problem and the RL question -------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The problem: where RL comes in}
\body
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.60\linewidth]{\fig{06_top_congested.png}}
\vfill
\raggedright
At the daily peak the three masterpiece rooms, Botticelli, Leonardo and Raphael, run at \emp{roughly
twice capacity} (red, past the dashed line) while the rest of the museum sits well below. \emp{The
question:} can reinforcement learning help us \emp{understand} this crowding and \emp{simulate} a
calmer, better-run Uffizi?
\end{frame}

% --- 4. The plan ------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The plan}
\body
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.4em}
\item \emp{Recreate the Uffizi} as a virtual model: every room, its size and doorways, plus a
\emp{value} for each (a Botticelli room is worth far more than a stairwell).
\item \emp{Generate the crowd}, today's arrivals and mix, about \emp{60\%} phone-first tourists,
\emp{30\%} ordinary visitors, \emp{10\%} art lovers. Validate it against the density maps (does it look
like the Uffizi today?), then read off today's \emp{revenue} and average \emp{welfare}.
\item \emp{Drop an RL agent} on the museum as-is: the optimal visitor today.
\item \emp{Intervene:} change hours, room access and pricing; re-simulate; keep only changes that hold
or raise revenue while lifting welfare, because a revenue-losing redesign is never adopted.
\item \emp{Re-run the RL agents} on the intervened museum: did the optimal visit improve for every kind
of visitor?
\end{enumerate}
\end{frame}

% --- 5. Scaling + masking ---------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The toy does not scale: deep RL and masking}
\body
The real museum \emp{breaks the table}: ninety-eight rooms, $\sim$100 moves per step, hundreds of state
numbers. The same Q-table could never fill, so we change algorithm, to a neural net (\emp{MaskablePPO};
PPO and DQN as controls).
\vfill
\begin{block}{Action masking: the rulebook}
From any room only a few moves are legal, yet the net has $\sim$100 outputs. We set every illegal move
to $-\infty$ before the softmax, so it gets exactly zero probability. So no gradient goes into the
moves that cannot happen; the agent only ever picks among the legal ones.
\end{block}
\vfill
\[ \pi(a\mid s)=\frac{e^{z_a}\,\mathbf{1}[a\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}{\sum_{b}e^{z_b}\,\mathbf{1}[b\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}. \]
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- 12. Which algorithm wins and why masking ------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Which algorithm wins and why masking matters}
\bodydense
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.66\linewidth]{\fig{15_algorithm_comparison.png}}
\vfill
\raggedright
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.3em}
\item Pale bars: the two no-booking baselines. Coloured: the three booking agents.
\item \emp{MaskablePPO} (dark blue) is best or tied in every cell and stable across seeds: the winner.
\item \emp{Unmasked PPO} (orange) \bad{collapses} at the packed tourist (\bad{2093}, below the $2680$
baseline): it wastes effort on illegal moves. Masking only matters at the hard cells, but there it
decides the result.
\item \emp{DQN} (green) is crowd-fragile and high-variance (the wide error bars): a max over noisy crowd
values overestimates, the bias the deterministic toy did not have.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% --- 13. Challenge 1 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{1: an unlearnable reward}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{The agent never left, squatting in one room until closing. Zeroing all reward for not exiting made
it \emp{worse}.}
{An all-or-nothing wipe is \emp{flat} almost everywhere, so the gradient is zero: nothing points to
the door.}
{Use a \emp{dense} egress signal: per-step pressure rising near closing and far from an exit, plus a
bonus for leaving.}
{Even a correct reward is useless if it is flat everywhere. Gradient descent needs a slope to follow,
so we built one in.}
\end{frame}

% --- 11. Challenge 2 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{2: it could not see what it was deciding}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{The rule ``stay until satisfied, then move'' never stabilised; dwell times wobbled at random.}
{The right action depended on value already taken from the room, \emp{not in the state}: a hidden
variable the agent could not see.}
{Add a per-room \emp{appreciation-progress} value to the state, so ``seen enough?'' becomes something
the policy can read.}
{Whatever the best move depends on has to live in the state. We had left it out, so no policy could
settle.}
\end{frame}

% --- 12. Challenge 3 --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{3: reward shaping that backfired}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{Well-meant terms backfired: a ``boredom'' penalty caused \emp{looping}, a harsh crowd penalty made a
\emp{fake art lover}.}
{Each reward term we added gave the agent a new way to cheat: looping beat exiting, a crowded
Botticelli scored worse than just skipping it.}
{\emp{Minimal} shaping: a \emp{gentle} crowd discount so a crowded masterpiece still beats skipping,
no boredom penalty, satiation alone stops lingering.}
{The agent games every term the laziest way it can. We stopped adding shaping until we had checked
what each term really paid for.}
\end{frame}

% --- 13. Challenge 4 (the best trick) ---------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{4: the do-nothing trap and the book-early trick}
\bodydense
\sdfl
{Given a free ``decline'' button, \emp{both} profiles declined all three masterpieces; book-early
stayed only \emp{half} learned.}
{Declining is safe now, booking pays only later: a do-nothing optimum; its reward lands hundreds of
steps away (credit assignment).}
{Make ``always books'' a \emp{type trait} with no escape hatch; add an \emp{immediate off-pace
penalty} proxying the distant cost.}
{A small penalty now, standing in for a cost hundreds of steps away, was what finally got it booking.
Nothing else we tried helped as much.}
\end{frame}

% --- 16. Conclusion ---------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Conclusion}
\body
We rebuilt the Uffizi, matched it to the real museum, then asked two questions: how to redesign the
building and how a single visitor should play it.
\vfill
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.7em}
\item \emp{The redesign:} eleven Pigovian measures lift welfare \good{$+31\%$} with revenue held.
\item \emp{The visitor:} the optimal agent (MaskablePPO) books early and beats its as-is ceiling at
every crowd level.
\item \emp{The lesson:} choosing what the agent decides was far harder than the algorithm itself;
action masking did more for us than any amount of tuning.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% --- A2. The as-is 8-number state -------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: the as-is state, eight numbers}
\body
The visitor's local view on the planned walk (natural units; the network sees each scaled to about
$[0,1]$):
\vfill
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.55em}
\item \emp{Room-value drain}: minutes absorbed vs the room's worth ($\sim$$3$ min per importance point).
\item \emp{Room value}: importance, $1$ to $10$.
\item \emp{Density}: how full the room is, percent of capacity.
\item \emp{On-target flag}: are you in the next planned must-see?
\item \emp{Plan completed}: share of must-sees finished.
\item \emp{Time of day}: minute of the $615$-minute day.
\item \emp{Egress slack}: spare minutes to reach an exit before closing.
\item \emp{Distance to next room}: doorways to the next must-see.
\end{enumerate}
\vfill
\centering \emp{At the door \texttt{A1}: $(0,\,0.22,\,0.30,\,0,\,0,\,0,\,0.99,\,0.10)$.}
\end{frame}

% --- A3. The RAMA 25-number state -------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: the RAMA state, twenty-five numbers}
\body
A two-phase episode (a booking screen, then the walk), grouped:
\vfill
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.55em}
\item \emp{Booking position} ($3$): phase flag, which of $4$ decisions, lead chosen (days).
\item \emp{Booking screen} ($4$): slots free at lead $1$/$7$/$21$/$35$ days (out of $8$).
\item \emp{Time of day} ($1$): minute of the $720$-minute day.
\item \emp{Windows held} ($3$): booked clock-times for Botticelli, Leonardo, Raphael.
\item \emp{Check-ins} ($3$): which masterpieces collected.
\item \emp{Access read} ($4$): is-masterpiece, unbooked, open-now, minutes-to-open.
\item \emp{Pace prior} ($3$): when it expects to reach each masterpiece (frozen).
\item \emp{Room tail} ($4$): drain, value, density, egress, as in the as-is state.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\centering \emp{Opening screen: $(1,0,1;\ 0.12,0.38,0.62,1;\ 0;\ \dots)$; the booking screen is the crux.}
\end{frame}

% --- A4. Actions and masking ------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: actions and action masking}
\large
\vfill
\textbf{Actions per setting}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1.0em}
\item Toy / as-is: \texttt{Discrete(2)}, stay or move on.
\item RAMA: \texttt{Discrete(4)}. Booking phase: a lead $\in\{1,7,21,35\}$ or book/decline. Walk phase:
stay or move on.
\end{itemize}
\vfill
\textbf{Masking (MaskablePPO).} Set the logits of illegal moves to $-\infty$ before the softmax, so they
get exactly zero probability:
\vfill
\[ \pi(a\mid s)=\frac{e^{z_a}\,\mathbf{1}[a\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}{\sum_{b}e^{z_b}\,\mathbf{1}[b\in\mathcal{A}(s)]}. \]
\vfill
\end{frame}

% --- A5. Reward functions ---------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: the reward functions}
\normalsize
\vfill
\textbf{Toy, per step.}
$\dfrac{\text{importance}\times\text{novelty}}{1+\alpha_A\,\text{density}^2}-\text{step cost}$, with
$\alpha_A=6$, step $0.5$, exit bonus $+15$, no-exit penalty $-50$ (hand-tuned shaping; the relative
sizes are what matter).
\vfill
\textbf{Full museum, per minute.} importance $\times$ crowd discount $\times$ novelty (satiation), with
a gentle crowd $\alpha=0.5$, plus closing-time pressure, a completion bonus and a small step cost.
\vfill
\textbf{Booking terms (RAMA only).} an \emp{immediate off-pace penalty} at commit time (a local proxy
for the distant cost of a badly-timed slot) plus a no-show penalty for a masterpiece booked but missed.
\end{frame}

% --- A16. Limitations and next steps ----------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Backup: limitations and where we would go next}
\small
\vfill
\textbf{One visitor, not a crowd.} What we optimise is a single sophisticated visitor's best response to
a \emp{fixed} crowd. It is not an equilibrium: if every visitor booked this way the good slots would
fill faster and the fill curve itself would move. Closing that loop, a mean-field game over the whole
population, is the real next step.
\vfill
\textbf{Fixes we did not run.} Our toy isolates the overestimation that makes DQN crowd-fragile, yet we
never tried the obvious cure, Double-DQN, on the full task. Two experiments would sharpen the story: a
fixed-heuristic booker (``always reserve a month out'') as a tougher baseline than no-booking, plus an
ablation that switches the off-pace penalty off to confirm that booking early is learned rather than
coaxed.
\vfill
\textbf{What is baked in.} The connoisseur always books (a trait, not a trick to win); lead times are
four discrete values; the fill curve is calibrated rather than fitted to real booking data; the policy
is a plain feedforward net carrying appreciation-progress, with $\gamma=0.999$. Welfare, finally, is the
simulator's own visitor-utility, itself a modelling choice rather than a settled social objective.
\vfill
\end{frame}
