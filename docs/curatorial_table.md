# Curatorial table: 14 secondary-attractor rooms

This document is the museum director's brief. For each of the 14
secondary-attractor rooms upstream of a masterpiece on the 2nd floor,
it specifies the concrete sensory interventions to install.

The orienting principle is total sensory immersion. The Vatican wins on
single-room iconography (Sistine, Stanze). The Uffizi has more variety
but no comparably transcendent single room (the Botticellis are crushed
by crowds). The opportunity is to make every secondary room a sensory
event so visitors leave thinking "every room was a world" rather than
"I came for four paintings and pushed through fifty corridors."

Each room engages 2-4 of the five sensory channels:

- **Visual** — projection-mapping, multimedia screens, restoration glass,
  theatrical lighting
- **Auditory** — period music, narration, ambient soundscape
- **Tactile** — replica handling stations, touch surfaces, rubbing panels
- **Olfactory** — period-appropriate scent
- **Live event** — scheduled curator talks, restorer at work, translator
  in residence

No room engages all five. The channels and content are unique per room
so visitors experience variety, not formula.

## Effect-size encoding

Each channel maps to a research-grounded boost on the simulator's
room-level importance and magnetism attributes. Importance affects
choice-to-enter; magnetism affects dwell time once inside.

| Channel | importance delta | magnetism delta |
|---|---|---|
| Visual screen | +1.0 | +0.5 |
| Audio narration / music | +0.5 | +0.7 |
| Tactile station | +0.3 | +0.7 |
| Olfactory atmosphere | +0.2 | +0.3 |
| Live event (timed) | +1.5 | +1.5 |

Deltas across active channels sum per room. Importance is capped at 10.0
to remain below masterpiece level.

## Zone 1 — Upstream of Botticelli

Visitors here are about to hit the Botticelli bottleneck. Holding them
in these rooms staggers the inflow into A11/A12.

### A4 — Giotto & Cimabue (4 channels)
The pivot from medieval hieratic style to Renaissance volumetric figures.
This room should be a "wow" moment, not a pass-through.

- **Visual:** dual-screen wall placing Cimabue's *Maestà* next to
  Giotto's *Maestà*, animating Giotto's invention of pictorial space.
- **Audio:** Notre-Dame School polyphony (Pérotin) at low volume — the
  music that existed when Cimabue was painting, just pre-Giotto.
- **Olfactory:** beeswax and incense — the smell of a 14th-century
  church, contextualizing the religious function of these panels.
- **Live event:** Wednesday and Saturday Giotto-panel restoration
  showcase. A real restorer works in a glass-fronted conservation booth.

### A7 — Lorenzo Monaco & Gentile da Fabriano (2 channels)
Gentile's *Adoration of the Magi* is a great International Gothic work
underrated outside specialist circles.

- **Visual:** projection-mapping of the *Adoration of the Magi* in
  animation — the procession moving, the gold leaf glinting under
  changing light.
- **Audio:** late-medieval Burgundian court music, matching the
  aristocratic context of the patrons (Palla Strozzi).

### A8 — Masaccio & Beato Angelico (3 channels)
This is the perspective revolution. Masaccio invented one-point
perspective in painting. The educational hook writes itself.

- **Visual:** interactive vanishing-point demonstration. The floor is
  marked with a vanishing point; visitors stand on it and a wall
  projection reveals how Masaccio's *Trinity* uses single-point
  perspective.
- **Tactile:** a physical Alberti perspective grid (string-and-frame
  device) visitors look through to "see what Masaccio saw."
- **Audio:** four-minute narration triggered on entry, explaining the
  revolution.

### A9 — Piero della Francesca, Uccello, F. Lippi (2 channels)
Three first-rank quattrocento masters. Uccello did the *Battle of San
Romano*; Piero is one of the greatest mathematician-painters of the
Renaissance.

- **Visual:** animated Uccello battle scenes; projected Piero geometric
  studies showing his mathematical underpinning.
- **Tactile:** 3D-printed Piero perfect-solid models visitors can pick
  up — the geometric primitives that underlie his figures.

### A10 — Pollaiolo (3 channels) — *holdback room*
This is the last room before Botticelli. Holding visitors here directly
staggers the Botticelli inflow. Highest leverage room in the museum
after the masterpieces themselves.

- **Live event:** hourly 10-minute curator talk (10:30, 11:30, 12:30,
  …) on "what Pollaiolo taught Botticelli." Each talk holds 25
  visitors. Scheduled to peak just before the Botticelli rush hours.
- **Audio:** anatomy-study narration linking Pollaiolo's muscular nude
  drawings to the figures Botticelli will paint in the next room.
- **Visual:** anatomy-study video animating Pollaiolo's *Battle of the
  Nudes* engraving, the foundation of Renaissance figure drawing.

## Zone 2 — Between Botticelli and Eastern Corridor

Visitors exiting Botticelli would otherwise rush toward Leonardo via the
corridor. These rooms catch them.

### A13 — Hugo van der Goes, Portinari Altarpiece (4 channels)
One of the great Northern Renaissance works in any Italian collection.
Currently buried as a between-Botticelli-and-Tribune room. This is
masterpiece-level treatment.

- **Visual:** Flemish landscape projection on the side walls — Bruges
  canals, snowy Flanders — transporting the visitor north as they
  approach the altarpiece. Side panels animate to highlight the donor
  portraits.
- **Audio:** Burgundian sacred music (Dufay's *Nuper rosarum flores*),
  the same musical world Hugo would have known.
- **Olfactory:** cold Northern pine resin. The smell of Flanders in
  winter.
- **Live event:** Wednesday-evening "Flemish in Florence" curator talk
  on why this work was commissioned and what it meant in Medici
  Florence.

### A14 — Uffizi Maps Terrace (2 channels)
A natural pause room with views of the Arno and Ponte Vecchio.
Currently a hallway. Lean into the sensory match to the outdoor
adjacency.

- **Visual:** animated historical Florence maps overlaid on the actual
  view through the windows.
- **Olfactory:** Tuscan herbs — rosemary, sage, citrus — sourced from
  gardens visible below.

### A18 — Mantegna, Bellini, Antonello da Messina (3 channels)
The Venetian counterpoint to Florence. Three major non-Florentine
quattrocento names in one room.

- **Visual:** split-screen Florentine line vs Venetian color, comparing
  the same compositional choices made differently north and south.
- **Audio:** Gabrieli Venetian brass polychoral — the San Marco
  basilica sound, physically immersive.
- **Tactile:** replica Mantegna engraving plate. Mantegna pioneered
  engraving and the copper texture is unlike anything else in the
  museum.

### A22 — Boudoir of Miniatures (3 channels)
Small, intimate, easily overlooked. Lean into its character as a
"curiosity room" rather than fighting it.

- **Visual:** magnified miniature projections at scale on the side
  walls so visitors can see what the eye cannot resolve at the actual
  miniatures.
- **Olfactory:** bergamot and iris — Catherine de' Medici's signature
  perfume scent, the cosmetics culture of Renaissance Florence.
- **Tactile:** replica miniatures under glass for handheld examination.

## Zone 3 — West detour off Eastern Corridor (pre-Leonardo)

At the A24 corridor visitors choose between going straight to Leonardo
(east) or detouring west through A25–A30. Make the west detour
irresistible so the Leonardo inflow staggers.

### A25 — Ghirlandaio (2 channels)
"Michelangelo's teacher" — instant narrative hook.

- **Visual:** multimedia on Ghirlandaio's workshop, with focus on the
  young Michelangelo apprenticing here.
- **Audio:** workshop ambient sounds — chisels, brushes, Florentine
  voices in 15th-century vernacular.

### A27 — Perugino (3 channels)
"Raphael's teacher" — symmetric narrative hook to A25.

- **Visual:** multimedia on Perugino's clean style and the young
  Raphael's apprenticeship.
- **Audio:** Umbrian folk music — Perugino was Umbrian, not Florentine,
  geographically distinct.
- **Live event:** weekly curator talk paired with A25 ("the teachers
  walking tour").

### A28 — Filippino Lippi + Piero di Cosimo (2 channels)
The quattrocento finale before the corridor continues.

- **Visual:** comparison Filippino Lippi vs his father Filippo Lippi —
  one of the rare father-son artistic dynasties displayed side by side.
- **Audio:** late-quattrocento Florentine secular music.

### A30 — Doryphoros (3 channels) — *no screens*
Greek sculpture. Deliberately no visual-screen intervention — the
sculpture is the visual. Tactile-first treatment, the only room of the
14 with this character.

- **Tactile:** tactile replica visitors run their hands over. Greek
  sculpture was meant to be felt.
- **Olfactory:** olive and Mediterranean herbs (laurel, thyme).
- **Audio:** ancient Greek aulos music.

---

## Zone 4 — Pre-Raphael holdback

### A36 — Hall of Ancient Inscriptions (3 channels)
The one room visitors pass through after Leonardo but before Raphael.
Holding them here staggers Raphael's inflow. Currently a transit room
(importance 3, magnetism 0.8). Lean into the literary character.

- **Tactile:** replica inscriptions for charcoal-rubbing. Visitors take
  a paper rubbing home as a souvenir.
- **Audio:** Latin recitation — an actor reading Cicero or Augustus's
  *Res Gestae*.
- **Live event:** rotating translator-in-residence. A Latin scholar
  interprets inscriptions on demand for visitors.
